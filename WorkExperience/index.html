SUNY Morrisville Peer Tutoring

Spring 2024-2025, Spring 2026

During my sophomore year at SUNY Morrisville, I applied to become a peer tutor at the tutoring center. My primary responsibilities as a peer tutor include:

  • Helping my peers succeed in their courses
  • Facilitating a learning minset in students
  • Documenting tutor sessions and tracking student progress
  • Performing database entries using the tutoring center's front-end application system

Additionally, I would continue to tutor courses as I completed them while obtaining my Bachelor's degree. During my last semester as a peer tutor, I offered tutoring services in 26 courses, many of which were courses in information technology and mathematics.


Being a peer tutor taught me a lot about how I can communicate my ideas to others effectively. Knowing the course material was not enough. I had to determine how to convey the information to the tutee such that they understand it.

SUNY Morrisville Game Programming Internship

Spring 2026

Overview

Completing a semester-long internship is required for almost every major at SUNY Morrisille. While most student look outward to find an internship from a company, I was concerned about improving the Game Programming major experience. I was the first year of graduates from Morrisville's Game Programming major, and I wanted to help shape the future of the major. When Morrisivlle announced that it will start offering on-campus internships, I took the opportunity to propose my internship to the CIT department.

Primary Responsibilities

My internship responsibilities were focused around solving some problem the department was facing or improving an existing product/service. My responsibilities can be broken into three categories: marketing, student support, and game development.

Marketing

SUNY Morrisville's Game Programming major had a massive marketing issue. If you were to ask people on campus they knew Morrisivlle had a game programming major, the majority likely wouldn't even know the major existed. In result, I would spend the majority of time marketing the major.


Most of my time marketing went towards poster development. Since many students were unaware of the game programming majoer, each poster created was to help inform students about the major. I was given a library of images and a camera to create the posters, and I chose Figma to design the layout for each poster.


My last accomplishment for marketing the major was the creation of the Game Programming Student Project Archive. This page shows everything that students have accomplished in the major, from their game projects in GameMaker Studio, Unity, and Unreal, to assets and projects they completed in Blender.

Student Support

With Morrisivlle's Game Progarmming major being so new, there was no central location for student resources. As time went on, this proved to be an increasinly larger problem as many of my peers would forget things and spend days relearing concepts. So, I set out to work on student support documentation and build a central hub to store these documents.


Morrisivlle's CIT department did not have something I could use to store documentation. In result, it was up to me to develop the storage location for all student resources. The CIT department set me up with an internal email which I used to develop a new Microsoft Team and a public GitHub account. While the Team is only available to students in the CIT department, you can find the code I added to the GitHub on C# fundamentals here.


Most of the documentation I created was centered around game development. This includes topics on how to use game engines like GameMaker Studio and Unity to asset software like Blender. I primarily focused on text-based documentation, but I also experimented with video documents using the recording software, OBS, and editing videos with CapCut.

Game Development

Part of the game programming internship was to give me free roam to build a video game. I landed on an demo game developed from another student for his final project and requested to expand on his game for my internship. I was given approval and started development on my forked GitHub repository.


The video game, Midnight Drifters, is a multiplayer racing game developed in Unity. My primary goal for the game was to learn multiiplayer architecture in a familiar game engine. However, I also wanted to finish the game and release it on Steam. Unfortunately, due to time restrictions and other internship responsibilities, I was not able to finish developing the video game. The game is playable, though, with known bugs and missing assets. You may play the game alone or with others by downloading it here.


I chose to implement the client-server multiplayer architecture due to its affordability and simplicity. Unity handles most of the networking through their script components and network manager singleton. All I had to do was customize the logic for starting as a host and joining a session as a client. You may find how I handled client/host sessions here.
